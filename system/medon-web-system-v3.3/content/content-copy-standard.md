# Medon Content & Copy Standard

## Why this exists

The old failure mode is “correct but generic”: technically optimized copy that sounds like category-average AI output.

This standard is a production process, not a bag of headline formulas.

## Gate 1 — Audience + intent

Before writing, answer:
- Who is this page for?
- What triggered the visit?
- What are they trying to decide/do?
- What do they already know?
- What are they skeptical about?
- What is the next sensible action?

## Gate 2 — Research + consensus / retrieval landscape

Map:
- live SERP intent/format where relevant;
- competitor consensus;
- obvious claims everyone already makes;
- buyer objections;
- first-party language;
- community/local language where useful;
- for important AIO/AI-search topics where practical: cited/source domains, source types, repeated entities/facts, likely subqueries/comparisons/follow-ups.

Competitor consensus tells us what **not** to simply rephrase. Retrieval research tells us where systems/users actually seek corroboration. Neither is permission to spam or fabricate consensus.

## Gate 3 — Same-site redundancy

Inspect the existing domain.

Ask:
- What has this site already answered?
- Which proof/data is already used?
- Is this page genuinely distinct?
- Would another page be stronger if expanded instead?

Do not publish keyword/city swaps.

## Gate 4 — Information gain

For important search/commercial pages define at least two concrete gains where possible.

Prefer at least one first-party gain:
- real customer question;
- call transcript insight;
- internal data;
- operational process;
- pricing/range;
- case/result;
- experienced failure mode;
- local delivery reality;
- genuine comparison.

Other valid gains:
- unanswered SERP question;
- decision framework;
- original synthesis;
- useful calculator/checklist;
- explicit trade-off;
- clearer classification.

No page is required to exist merely because a keyword has volume.

Legal/contact/utility pages are exempt from this “novel insight” requirement.

## Gate 4.5 — “Only we could make this” test

For important pages, try to include at least one meaningful element that this business/expert/data source is unusually qualified to produce:
- own data;
- own method;
- own tool/calculator;
- direct operational experience;
- real process/pricing constraints;
- original media;
- documented customer evidence.

Do not fake uniqueness when none exists. A better answer may be consolidation or a different content format.

## Gate 5 — Proof

List what only this business can credibly say.

Proof hierarchy:
1. verified first-party result/data;
2. documented case;
3. concrete process/operational detail;
4. verified experience/credential;
5. real customer language;
6. transparent limitation.

Proof beats promise.

If proof is missing, do not fake confidence.

## Gate 6 — Message and hook

Generate multiple candidates.

A strong hook should:
- make the topic clear immediately;
- feel relevant to the intended buyer;
- express a valuable outcome or important problem;
- create contrast/curiosity only after clarity.

Useful heuristics:
- “Yay without Boo”;
- specific problem → desirable result;
- damaging admission → credible strength;
- concrete decision question;
- contrast with common alternative.

These are optional tools, not mandatory templates.

Reject hooks with:
- delay;
- confusion;
- irrelevance;
- empty curiosity;
- unsupported superlatives.

## Gate 7 — Human specificity

Delete generic filler such as:
- “skreddersydde løsninger”;
- “vi brenner for”;
- “høy kvalitet”;
- “din pålitelige partner”;
- “helhetlige løsninger”;
- “revolusjonerende”;
- “transformativ”.

Keep a phrase only when the next sentence proves what it means.

Write moments, not abstractions.

Instead of:
> Få en enklere arbeidshverdag.

Prefer:
> Dere slipper å samle menyønsker, koordinere leverandører og løse lunsjproblemer hver morgen.

## Gate 8 — Show, don't tell

Do not merely claim:
- easier;
- faster;
- more leads;
- better service;
- higher quality.

Describe the observable experience/result.

## Gate 9 — Clarity

Prefer:
- short sentences;
- simple words;
- active voice;
- specific verbs;
- concrete nouns;
- one idea per paragraph.

Aim for language a busy reader understands instantly. Do not dumb down technical terms that are necessary for precision.

## Gate 10 — Nordic credibility

Hormozi-style mechanics are not a voice.

Avoid:
- fake urgency;
- fake scarcity;
- exaggerated bonus values;
- American infomercial tone;
- uncontrolled guarantees;
- theatrical claims.

Use restraint, evidence and specificity.

## Fun facts / fact blocks

Verified facts can be useful content when they improve understanding, specificity or memorability. This is especially relevant on Medon-owned topical/authority sites.

Do not add trivia for decoration. Every fact must be:
- true and sourced;
- relevant to the page/audience;
- specific enough to be useful;
- interpreted with “why it matters” when the implication is not obvious.

Time-sensitive facts must be reverified before publication. Third-party facts provide context; they do not become proof of a customer's own results.

See `content/fun-facts.md`.

## CTA rule

CTA must clearly state:
- what action to take;
- what happens next;
- why that step is useful.

Do not default to “Book et møte” if a lower-friction next step better matches awareness.

## Functionality can be content

Before defaulting to another article/page, ask whether the user's job is better solved by:
- calculator/estimator;
- assessment;
- configurator;
- checklist generator;
- comparison tool;
- search/filter;
- booking/action flow;
- interactive data/visualization.

Quality is not word count. Effort can be code, data, experience, utility or execution.

## Reputation / corroboration

For important commercial, local and especially high-stakes topics, distinguish what the business says about itself from what credible independent sources can verify. Lack of external reputation is not automatically negative.

## Lead magnets / mini-offers

Use only when they improve the journey.

Useful mechanisms:
- reveal a problem;
- sample/trial;
- one step of a larger process.

Qualify before expensive free fulfillment.

## Anti-slop final test

Ask:

> Could a competitor publish a near-identical version tomorrow by swapping the company name, city and a few examples?

If yes, the page fails.

Then ask:

> What useful thing would disappear from the web/site if this page disappeared?

If the answer is weak, do not polish the prose. Fix the page premise.

## Output before production copy

Every important page must have:
- Page Brief;
- proof/evidence list;
- information gain;
- 3–10 hook candidates depending on importance;
- selected angle and reason.
